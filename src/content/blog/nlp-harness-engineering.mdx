Imperial's NLP course runs SemEval-2022 Task 4 as a competition: detect patronising and condescending language aimed at vulnerable people. 340 of us, one leaderboard. Writing the detection systems and tuning the models by hand, I came 10th.

Then I built an agent system to do the same task. No human in the loop, one shot, no prompt engineering. Then I put myself back in, directing the agents and checking what they produced.

## The numbers

- Me alone: 10/340
- My system alone: 22/340
- Me and the system: 1/340

The winning run was a context-enriched DeBERTa with focal loss, a community-context prefix, a five-seed ensemble and a tuned threshold, at a 9.5:1 class imbalance. F1 0.61, against a RoBERTa baseline of 0.48.

The middle number is the one people skip. Left alone, the agent system was above average and nothing more. It was beaten by 21 students working by hand, including me. If you had shown me only that result I would have concluded that agents were not ready for this.

The gap between 22nd and 1st is not a better model. It is the same model, wrapped in something that catches it when it goes wrong. I wrote the guardrails and the logging because I could not otherwise tell whether an output was reasoned or invented, and I could not improve what I could not see.

I did not have a word for this at the time. The industry settled on one later.

The part worth taking seriously is the size of that gap. Twenty-one places, on the same task, with the same model underneath. Everyone is comparing models. Nobody I was competing against was measuring what the wrapper was worth.
